**Quick summary:** the ideal title tag length is 50 to 60 characters, roughly 580 to 600 pixels on desktop. The 51-to-55-character range is the one Google rewrites the least: only 40% of titles in that range were altered, compared to nearly 100% of titles over 70 characters.

The numbers come from an analysis of 81,000 title tags by [Zyppy](https://zyppy.com/seo/google-title-rewrite-study/)'s Cyrus Shepard, who also cross-referenced title length with real Google traffic. The topic connects directly with what we already covered in [how to write titles and meta descriptions that increase CTR](/en/blog/como-escrever-titulos-e-meta-descricoes-que-aumentam-o-ctr).

Now, here's the detail almost everyone gets wrong: Google has no character limit for the title. The limit is a display limit, not an indexing limit. And that difference changes how you should write titles.

## Does Google have a character limit for the title?

Google doesn't set any fixed character limit for the title tag. What exists is a display limit: on desktop, search shows about 600 pixels of the title, which works out to roughly 60 characters. Past that, Google truncates with an ellipsis or rewrites the title on its own.

On mobile, it's a different story. Zyppy's measurements showed titles displayed at 70 to 80 characters in mobile results, and on some devices Google showed titles at nearly 1,000 pixels wide. Since more than 60% of search traffic comes from mobile, the famous "60-character limit" is far less rigid than it sounds.

For indexing, it's even less of a constraint. Google itself confirmed, on an episode of the Search Off the Record podcast, that its internal limit is enormous, and independent experiments have shown Google indexing titles with more than 1,000 characters. In other words: the keywords in your title count for ranking even when they don't appear on screen.

Does that mean you can stuff your title with keywords at no cost? No. The cost shows up elsewhere: in the rewrite.

## How often does Google rewrite titles?

Google rewrote titles across every length range in the Zyppy study, but the frequency varies sharply depending on length:

* Titles of 1 to 5 characters: rewritten in more than 96% of cases, usually with Google adding words for context
* Titles of 51 to 55 characters: rewritten in about 40% of cases, the lowest rate in the whole study
* Titles over 70 characters: rewritten in nearly 100% of cases, because no title that long fits within the 600 desktop pixels

The curve is U-shaped. A title that's too short is too vague, and Google "fills in" for you. A title that's too long overflows the space, and Google cuts or swaps it. The bottom of the U, where your title has the best chance of appearing exactly as you wrote it, sits between 51 and 55 characters.

There's also a side effect that doesn't get talked about much: since display limits differ by device, the same URL can show one title on desktop and a different one on mobile, sometimes with slightly different meanings. If message control matters to your brand, that's one more argument for staying in the safe range.

## Do short or long titles drive more traffic?

Short and medium titles beat long ones in every study available so far, though none of them prove cause and effect:

* [Backlinko analyzed 4 million search results and found CTR](https://seoliddi.tech/blog-seo-tecnico/analise-ctr-organico/) about 36% higher for titles between 15 and 40 characters, compared to titles outside that range
* [Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/codeascraft/seo-title-tag-optimization/)'s engineering team ran controlled tests across thousands of pages, and the shorter-title variants brought in more total clicks than the longer ones
* Zyppy itself, in a dataset of about 250,000 URLs, saw traffic peak at titles of 55 to 60 characters, with overly short titles at the bottom of the table

Etsy's theory for the result is a good one: a short, focused title tends to stick closer to the user's search intent. Every extra word dilutes perceived relevance. Google itself recommends, in its [official documentation](https://support.google.com/docs/thread/313875309/t%C3%ADtulo-e-subt%C3%ADtulo-sem-ordem-ficando-presente-em-todo-o-texto-mesmo-realizando-a-separa%C3%A7%C3%A3o-certa?hl=pt), removing repetitive, boilerplate text from the title when it doesn't add value.

It's worth noting the caveat Cyrus Shepard himself makes: correlation isn't causation. It's possible that 55-character titles perform well simply because experienced SEO professionals already write in that format. Still, when Backlinko, Etsy, and Zyppy point in the same direction with different methodologies, ignoring the pattern is just stubbornness.

## How do you apply this to your own titles?

Applying these numbers day to day comes down to four practical decisions:

1. **Aim for 51 to 55 characters** when you want to minimize rewrites. Up to 60 is still safe territory for desktop display.
2. **Put the main keyword at the beginning.** If Google truncates it, the important part survives. And a user scanning results reads the first words first.
3. **Cut boilerplate.** That "| Company X Blog | Technology Solutions" at the end of every title eats up pixels and convinces nobody to click.
4. **Mostly mobile audience? You have some slack.** You can go up to around 70 characters before truncation becomes a problem. Just don't go too short: a vague title gets rewritten on any screen, the same kind of technical factor that weighs on a site's [overall Google ranking](/en/blog/como-melhorar-o-ranking-do-seu-site-no-google-dicas-praticas).

The tedious part is measuring this at scale. Counting characters one by one doesn't scale, and character isn't even the right unit: "WWW" takes up far more pixels than "iii," so two 58-character titles can meet very different fates on the SERP. Tools like Screaming Frog calculate pixel width for every title in a crawl, but they stop at measurement. They tell you if the title fits, not if it's good.

## Summary: the numbers that matter

* **50 to 60 characters** (580 to 600 pixels) is the ideal display range on desktop
* **51 to 55 characters** is the range with the lowest rewrite rate from Google: 40%
* **Above 70 characters**, a rewrite is nearly guaranteed: close to 100%
* **Mobile shows more**: 70 to 80 characters, with cases of nearly 1,000 pixels
* **There's no indexing limit**: Google reads and ranks words from titles over 1,000 characters
* **Titles of 15 to 40 characters** had 36% higher CTR in the Backlinko study, and the Zyppy dataset's traffic peak sat at 55 to 60
