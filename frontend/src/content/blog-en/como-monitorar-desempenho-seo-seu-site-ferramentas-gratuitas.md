In the digital age, your online presence isn't just a storefront — it's your main acquisition channel. But having a beautiful website isn't enough.

SEO (Search Engine Optimization) is the difference between a site that generates consistent results and one that stays lost on Google's second page. But how do you know if your strategies are working without spending a fortune on premium tools?

Let's look at how to effectively monitor your [SEO performance](/en/blog/como-criar-conteudo-seo-friendly-que-converte/) with completely free tools, turning data into decisions that drive your online growth.

### What Is SEO and Why You Need to Monitor It

SEO is the set of practices aimed at improving your site's position in the organic results of search engines like Google, Bing, and Yahoo. It's not just about attracting more traffic, but the right traffic — visitors with the potential to become customers or loyal followers.

A [Sistrix study](https://www.sistrix.com/) found that the first result on Google gets 28.5% of clicks, while the tenth result gets just 2.5%. That means moving up even a single position can mean a significant increase in organic traffic.

Ongoing SEO monitoring lets you:

-   Identify what's working and what needs adjustment
    
-   Understand how visitors interact with your content
    
-   Catch technical issues before they hurt your ranking
    
-   Track competitor performance and adapt
    
-   Demonstrate ROI to stakeholders or clients
    

### Essential Metrics and KPIs for Monitoring SEO Performance

Before we dive into the tools, we need to understand what to measure. Metrics are quantitative data points you can collect, while KPIs (Key Performance Indicators) are specific metrics that align with your objectives.

### Visibility Metrics

-   **Search result positions**: where your site appears for relevant keywords
    
-   **Impressions**: how many times your site showed up in results
    
-   **Clicks**: how many times users clicked through to your site from results
    

### Engagement Metrics

-   **Bounce rate**: the percentage of visitors who leave the site after viewing only one page
    
-   **Average time on page**: how long visitors spend consuming your content
    
-   **Pages per session**: how many pages each visitor views on average
    

### Conversion Metrics

-   **Conversion rate**: the percentage of visitors who complete a desired action
    
-   **Value per visitor**: how much revenue each visitor generates on average
    
-   **Acquisition cost**: how much it costs to attract each new customer through SEO
    

### Free Tools for SEO Monitoring

### Google Search Console

Google Search Console is Google's official tool for webmasters and is absolutely essential for any SEO strategy.

**Main features:**

-   **Performance report**: shows clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position
    
-   **Index coverage**: identifies which pages are indexed and which have issues
    
-   **Experience enhancements**: analysis of [Core Web Vitals](/en/blog/o-impacto-dos-core-web-vitals-no-seo-um-guia-completo/) and mobile usability
    
-   **Links**: tracks who's linking to your site
    
-   **URL Inspection**: lets you request indexing for new pages
    

Search Console also alerts you to critical issues, such as manual penalties or security problems.

### Google Analytics

While Universal Analytics is being phased out, Google Analytics 4 (GA4) remains a powerful, free tool for traffic analysis.

**Main features:**

-   **Acquisition reports**: identify where your traffic comes from
    
-   **User behavior**: track how visitors navigate the site
    
-   **Conversions**: measure valuable actions like sign-ups or purchases
    
-   **Audience segmentation**: split visitors into groups for deeper analysis
    
-   **Real-time view**: shows what's happening right now
    

### Bing Webmaster Tools

Don't overlook Bing! With around 6% global market share, Microsoft's search engine can represent a meaningful slice of your traffic.

**Main features:**

-   **SEO reports**: shows SEO issues and suggestions
    
-   **Keyword analysis**: reveals which terms are driving traffic
    
-   **Crawl diagnostics**: identifies issues preventing crawling
    
-   **Backlink Explorer**: analyzes inbound links
    
-   **Mobile URL checker**: tests mobile device compatibility
    

### SEMrush (Free Version)

SEMrush offers a limited free version that's still valuable for basic analysis.

**Main free-version features:**

-   **Domain analysis**: an overview of site performance
    
-   **Keyword research**: 10 queries per day
    
-   **Competitor analysis**: a basic study of competitors
    
-   **Site audits**: technical issue checks (limited)
    
-   **Position tracking**: monitoring for a limited number of keywords
    

### Ubersuggest

Built by [Neil Patel](https://neilpatel.com/), Ubersuggest offers surprisingly complete features in its free version.

**Main features:**

-   **Site overview**: basic performance metrics
    
-   **Keyword research**: search volume and ranking difficulty
    
-   **Backlink analysis**: your inbound link profile
    
-   **Content analysis**: suggestions for improving existing pages
    
-   **Site audit**: identifying technical issues
    

### Screaming Frog SEO Spider (Free Version)

This desktop tool lets you crawl up to 500 URLs for free, offering detailed technical analysis.

**Main features:**

-   **Technical issue identification**: finds 404 errors, redirects, and canonicalization issues
    
-   **Meta tag analysis**: checks titles, descriptions, and heading tags
    
-   **Structured data extraction**: analyzes schema markup
    
-   **Broken link checking**: identifies non-functional links
    
-   **Sitemap generation**: automatically creates [XML sitemap files](/en/blog/como-criar-um-sitemap-melhorar-indexacao-site/)
    

### Monitoring User Behavior

### Hotjar (Free Basic Plan)

[Hotjar](https://www.hotjar.com/) lets you visualize how users interact with your site, offering valuable insight into the user experience.

**Main features:**

-   **Heatmaps**: show where users click, move their cursor, and scroll
    
-   **Session recordings**: let you watch real user navigation
    
-   **Conversion funnels**: identify where users drop off in a process
    
-   **Surveys**: collect direct feedback from visitors
    
-   **A/B testing**: compare different versions of page elements
    

### Microsoft Clarity

[Microsoft Clarity](https://clarity.microsoft.com/), which is free, offers features similar to Hotjar with no pageview limits.

**Main features:**

-   **Heatmaps**: visualize click and scroll behavior
    
-   **Session recordings**: show the complete user journey
    
-   **Frustration insights**: identify rage clicks, excessive scrolling, and other frustration signals
    
-   **Segmentation**: filter data by source, device, and behavior
    

### Effective Monitoring Strategies

### Establish a Baseline

Before implementing any changes, document your site's current state. This lets you measure the real impact of your optimizations.

### Monitor Competitors

Use tools like SEMrush or Ubersuggest to track competitor performance — see also our guide on [SEO competitor analysis](/en/blog/analise-de-concorrentes-em-seo-como-identificar-e-superar-seus-competidores-nos-resultados-de-busca/). When a competitor moves sharply up or down in the rankings, investigate what changed.

### Build a Custom Dashboard

[Looker Studio](https://lookerstudio.google.com/) (formerly Google Data Studio) lets you build free dashboards that consolidate data from multiple sources. This makes it easier to visualize and analyze your most important metrics.

### Set Up a Monitoring Routine

-   **Daily**: check basic metrics like traffic and positions
    
-   **Weekly**: analyze keyword trends and user behavior
    
-   **Monthly**: run technical audits and update your strategy
    
-   **Quarterly**: conduct an in-depth performance review and compare against goals
    

### Focus on Metrics That Matter

As [Peter Drucker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Drucker) said, "What gets measured gets managed." But the reverse is also true — not everything that can be measured should be managed. Focus on the metrics that actually impact your goals.

### Interpreting the Data to Make Decisions

Data on its own has no value. The real power lies in interpretation and the actions you take based on it.

### Drop in Organic Traffic

**Possible causes:**

-   Google algorithm update
    
-   Technical issues like 404 errors or deindexing
    
-   Loss of valuable backlinks
    
-   Increased competition
    

**Diagnostic tools:**

-   Google Search Console (check impressions and clicks)
    
-   SEMrush or Ubersuggest (keyword analysis)
    
-   Screaming Frog (technical issues)
    

### High Bounce Rate

**Possible causes:**

-   Content that doesn't match search intent
    
-   Slow page loading
    
-   Poor mobile experience
    
-   Problematic design or usability
    

**Diagnostic tools:**

-   Google Analytics (to identify problem pages)
    
-   [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) (loading speed)
    
-   Hotjar or Microsoft Clarity (user behavior)
    

### Low Conversion Rate

**Possible causes:**

-   Unclear calls to action
    
-   A complicated conversion process
    
-   Insufficient trust
    
-   An unconvincing value proposition
    

**Diagnostic tools:**

-   Google Analytics (conversion funnels)
    
-   Hotjar (heatmaps and recordings)
    
-   Microsoft Clarity (frustration signals)
