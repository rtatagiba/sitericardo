# SEO Consulting vs. Agency: What's the Difference?

**Quick summary:** SEO consulting diagnoses, prioritizes, and directs. An SEO (or digital marketing) agency executes. Consulting delivers a plan and tracks the implementation carried out by the client's own team. An agency takes the plan and executes it themselves. These are complementary models, not competing ones, and the right choice comes down to one simple question: do you have people to execute or not?

## What changes in the delivery model?

The most concrete difference between consulting and an agency is who does the operational work.

In consulting, the consultant analyzes the site, surfaces the problems, cross-references data from tools like [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/), [Screaming Frog](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/), and [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) with the business context, and delivers [a prioritized action plan](/en/blog/o-que-e-consultoria-de-seo/). Then they follow the implementation with periodic meetings, adjust course when the data shows something unexpected, and answer the team's technical questions. But the client's own team does the implementing: the developer fixes the technical errors, the writer produces the content, the marketing analyst tracks the reports.

At an agency, the package is more complete on the execution side. The agency receives the brief (or builds it themselves) and handles everything: audit, content production, technical fixes, reporting, and sometimes even paid media and social media management. The client gets the result, not the process.

That difference looks small, but it changes everything. It changes who ends up hiring, changes the price, changes how fast implementation happens, and changes who's left holding the knowledge at the end of the project.

## When is an agency the better choice?

An agency makes the most sense when the company has no in-house digital marketing team. That's the case for [many small and mid-size businesses](/en/blog/como-fazer-seo-para-pequenas-empresas-um-guia-completo/): the owner handles sales, has a site someone built two years ago, and there's no analyst, writer, or developer on staff.

In that scenario, hiring consulting creates a practical problem: the consultant delivers an excellent plan, but there's nobody to execute it. The audit report becomes a pretty PDF nobody implements. An agency solves that because it does the executing itself.

It also makes sense when the company needs to scale quickly across multiple channels. Agencies offer teams with specialists in technical SEO, content, link building, and analytics, all operating at once. Building that team in-house would take months and cost more.

The catch: many agencies operate with standardized packages. The client gets locked into a monthly delivery flow (X posts, Y technical fixes, Z reports) that doesn't always reflect what the site actually needs. A good agency customizes. A mediocre agency applies the same package to everyone.

## When does consulting make more sense?

Consulting fits best in three scenarios.

First: the company already has a team. There's a developer, a writer, maybe even a marketing analyst. What's missing is someone to step back and say "the problem is here, this is the priority, start there." Hiring an agency in that case means paying for execution the internal team could already handle — it just needed direction.

Second: the problem is strategic, not operational. [Traffic dropped](/en/blog/como-melhorar-o-ranking-do-seu-site-no-google-dicas-praticas/) after a Google update. Or the company is losing visibility to [AI Overviews](/en/blog/a-oficializacao-do-grounding-o-futuro-do-trafego-organico-segundo-o-google/) and [ChatGPT](/en/blog/como-aparecer-no-chatgpt-guia-aeo-geo/). Or [organic traffic is growing but not converting](/en/blog/quatro-alavancas-receita-busca-google/). These are diagnostic problems. An agency might eventually solve them, but first someone needs to understand what's actually happening, and that's the consultant's job.

Third: the company already has an agency, but feels like it's missing direction. This is more common than it looks. The agency delivers reports every month, the numbers seem reasonable, but the business owner doesn't know whether any of it is actually moving the needle. The consultant steps in as a review layer: [evaluates the agency's work](/en/blog/como-monitorar-desempenho-seo-seu-site-ferramentas-gratuitas/), identifies what's working, what isn't, and where the investment pays off most.

## Can you combine both models?

You can, and it works well when the scopes are clear.

The most common model: the consultant does the initial diagnosis and builds the strategic plan. The agency receives the plan and executes it. The consultant checks in periodically to make sure execution stays aligned with the strategy.

It works poorly when both end up with overlapping scope and nobody knows who decides what. If the consultant and the agency disagree on priority, the client is stuck in the middle not knowing who to follow. That's why the arrangement needs to be explicit from the start: who does the diagnosis, who sets priorities, who executes, who measures.

Another model that works: consulting first, followed by an agency. The consultant works for 2 to 3 months, does the full diagnosis, builds the plan with well-defined deliverables, and then the company hires an agency to execute the plan. The consultant may or may not continue tracking progress.

## The most common mistake: hiring an agency when the problem is strategic

The scenario repeats itself often. A company notices its site isn't performing, hires an agency "to fix the SEO," and 6 months later is unhappy because traffic didn't climb as expected.

What happened: the agency started executing without diagnosing. It produced content without in-depth keyword research. It optimized pages that weren't a priority. It delivered monthly reports with metrics that look good (impressions went up!) but didn't move revenue.

The problem wasn't execution. It was direction.

That's not necessarily the agency's fault. Most agencies are built to execute, not to diagnose deeply. Not because they don't know how, but because their business model is scale: serving many clients with standardized processes. Deep, case-by-case diagnosis doesn't scale.

The opposite also happens, though it's rarer: hiring consulting when the problem is purely a matter of manpower. If [the site needs 50 new articles](/en/blog/como-criar-conteudo-seo-friendly-que-converte/) and the diagnosis is already done, paying a consultant to oversee the production of each article is a waste. Hire a writer or a content agency instead.

## How do you decide?

The decision comes down to two questions:

Do you know what needs to be done? If yes, it's an execution problem. Agency or in-house team.

You don't know what's wrong, or you know but don't know where to start? That's a diagnosis problem. Consulting.

If the answer is "I don't even know what to ask," consulting is almost always the better first step. It costs less than [6 months of an agency working blind](/en/blog/como-calcular-seu-orcamento-de-seo-um-guia-completo-para-empresas-brasileiras/) and delivers clarity before you commit budget to execution.
