Topical authority is the recognition a site, blog, or professional profile earns on a specific subject when it covers that topic in depth, consistently, and with content pieces that connect to each other, rather than publishing isolated posts on random topics. Google and generative AI use that recognition as a trust signal: a domain with ten connected posts about local SEO carries more weight, for a question about local SEO, than a domain with a single generic post about digital marketing, even if the two individual pieces are similar in quality.

That recognition also builds on the [shift to semantic SEO](/en/blog/a-mudanca-para-o-seo-semantico-o-que-os-vetores-significam-para-sua-estrategia/): models that represent content as vectors of meaning, not just keywords, see more clearly when multiple posts from the same domain reinforce the same semantic field.

The practical unit that builds topical authority is the content cluster, also called a pillar-and-cluster structure. A pillar post covers the broad topic (e.g., "Local SEO: the complete guide"), and satellite posts dig into specific subtopics (e.g., "Google Business Profile," "local citations," "LocalBusiness schema"), each one linking back to the pillar and to each other. It's this web of internal links, more than the raw number of posts, that signals complete coverage of the subject.

## Why does topical authority matter more in the age of AI?

Generative AI decides what to cite by evaluating not just the isolated snippet, but the authority context of the domain it comes from, a behavior documented in the [academic paper that defined Generative Engine Optimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735). A model that's already "seen" the same site show up as a consistent source across multiple questions on a topic tends to treat that domain as a more reliable reference than a site that appears once, in isolation.

This mechanism connects directly to [E-E-A-T](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness), the criterion Google has used for years to evaluate content quality and which now functions, in practice, as a technical filter for AI citation: the consistency of the entity behind the content, across every channel, has become a ranking signal. A well-built content cluster is the most direct way to demonstrate that expertise verifiably, article after article, not just on an "About" page.

## How do you build a content cluster (pillar and satellites)?

A content cluster is the set formed by a pillar post, which covers a broad topic in depth, and several satellite posts, which dig into specific subtopics and link back to the pillar. Building one follows four steps:

Choose the pillar topic. It needs to be broad enough to spawn multiple subtopics, but specific enough that it doesn't compete with its own satellites for the same search. "Local SEO" works as a pillar; "Google" doesn't, it's too broad.

Map the subtopics that already have demand or business relevance. Each subtopic becomes a candidate satellite post.

Write the pillar as a reference page: longer, covering the topic end to end, with links to each satellite as soon as it's published.

Write each satellite with full focus on its subtopic, without trying to repeat what the pillar already covers, following the same principles as [SEO-friendly content that converts](/en/blog/como-criar-conteudo-seo-friendly-que-converte/), and always with at least one link back to the pillar and, when it makes sense, to neighboring satellites.

## How many posts does it take for Google and AI to recognize authority?

There's no magic number officially documented by Google or by any generative AI, and any claim that "publish X posts and you'll earn authority" should be treated as market rule of thumb, not verified fact. The most reliable signal isn't quantity, it's coverage: a cluster with 6 to 10 satellite posts well connected to a pillar tends to demonstrate more depth than 30 standalone posts with no links between them, because the web of internal links is what communicates "this domain has mapped the entire subject," not the total number of published files.

## What's the difference between topical authority and domain authority?

Domain authority is a generic metric, popularized by tools like Moz and [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/domain-rating/), that estimates a site's overall strength based mainly on backlinks, without distinguishing by subject. Topical authority is subject-specific: a site can have moderate domain authority and still be treated as a strong reference in a narrow niche, if coverage of that niche is consistent and connected.

For generative AI, that difference matters more than it does for traditional Google. A model answering "what's the best local SEO tool" isn't evaluating a domain's overall strength, it's evaluating whether that domain has repeatedly and coherently demonstrated that it understands local SEO specifically. A small, focused blog can beat a large, generic domain in that contest, because topical density outweighs site size, the same kind of comparison that drives good [competitor analysis in SEO](/en/blog/analise-de-concorrentes-em-seo-como-identificar-e-superar-seus-competidores-nos-resultados-de-busca/).

## Summary: the topical authority checklist

Define the pillar before writing any satellite, not after. Write satellites that each answer ONE subtopic, with no overlap in primary keyword between them. Link each satellite back to the pillar as soon as it's published, and periodically review whether the pillar has had time to link every existing satellite. Treat post count as a weak proxy: the link web between posts is the real signal of complete coverage that Google and AI recognize as authority.

A domain can have dozens of posts with no clear topical authority if they never connect to each other. And it can build authority on a specific subject with a modest number of posts, as long as the pillar-and-satellite structure is actually implemented, not just in the writer's head.
