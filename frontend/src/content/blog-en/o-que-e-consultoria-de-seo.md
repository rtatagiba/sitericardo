# What Is SEO Consulting?

**Quick summary:** SEO consulting is a diagnostic and strategic-direction service. The consultant analyzes the site, identifies what's blocking organic growth, builds a prioritized action plan, and follows through on execution. The difference from a tutorial or course is that the diagnosis starts from the reality of your site and your market, not a generic example. The difference from an agency is that the consultant guides, but your own team (or partners you choose) does the executing.

## What does an SEO consultant actually do day to day?

SEO consulting is, above all, diagnostic work. The consultant looks at the site, the search data, [the competitors](/en/blog/analise-de-concorrentes-em-seo-como-identificar-e-superar-seus-competidores-nos-resultados-de-busca/), and the client's business, and answers a question that sounds simple but almost never has an obvious answer: why isn't this site showing up anymore?

The work starts with a technical audit. Tools like [Screaming Frog](https://www.screamingfrog.co.uk/seo-spider/), [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/), and [Google Search Console](https://search.google.com/search-console/about) reveal the site's real state: pages Google never indexed, crawl errors, keyword cannibalization (two pages competing for the same search), load speed, internal link structure. That technical diagnosis is the foundation, but it's not the whole picture.

The second layer is strategic. The consultant cross-references the technical data with what the business actually needs. An online store with 3,000 products and a blog with 15 articles are completely different problems, even though both "need to improve their SEO." The action plan for each looks nothing alike.

And the third layer — the one that separates real consulting from an automated checklist — is prioritization. No site fixes everything at once. The consultant decides what to do first, what can wait, and what simply isn't worth the effort. That call depends on context no tool can supply on its own.

## What problems does consulting solve that a tutorial doesn't?

SEO tutorials exist by the thousands. There's excellent, free content on [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/), the [Ahrefs blog](https://ahrefs.com/blog/), [Moz](https://moz.com/beginners-guide-to-seo). So why would anyone pay for consulting?

Because the tutorial teaches the concept. Consulting applies the concept to your specific case.

Think of it this way: you can watch 20 videos on how to diagnose an electrical problem in your house. But when the wall starts heating up, you call an electrician. Not because they know something the videos don't teach, but because they know how to look at your wiring and decide what to do right now, in this house, with this breaker panel.

SEO works the same way. The most common problems that bring clients to consulting aren't a lack of general knowledge. They are:

Traffic dropped and nobody knows whether it was a Google update, a technical issue, or cannibalization. The tutorial explains each cause separately. The consultant looks at the data and isolates which one is actually at play in your case.

The company has a marketing team, but the team doesn't know how to prioritize. [Fix Core Web Vitals](/en/blog/o-impacto-dos-core-web-vitals-no-seo-um-guia-completo/)? Create new content? Clean up old pages? All good ideas, but doing everything at once doesn't work with a [limited budget](/en/blog/como-calcular-seu-orcamento-de-seo-um-guia-completo-para-empresas-brasileiras/). The consultant builds the sequence.

Generative AI has started eating into traffic. [Google's AI Overviews](/en/blog/a-oficializacao-do-grounding-o-futuro-do-trafego-organico-segundo-o-google/), [ChatGPT](/en/blog/como-aparecer-no-chatgpt-guia-aeo-geo/), Perplexity. The site ranks on Google's first page, but the click doesn't come anymore because the answer already shows up in the AI box. That's a new problem most tutorials still don't cover well, and it takes case-by-case analysis to understand the real impact.

## What size of company is SEO consulting for?

SEO consulting isn't exclusive to big companies. But it's not for everyone either.

It makes sense to hire a consultant when the company already has a working site, already generates some traffic (even if not much), and has someone internally who can execute recommendations. That could be a developer, a writer, a marketing analyst. The consultant provides direction, but needs hands to implement it.

It doesn't make sense when the company doesn't have a validated product yet, when the site is a single page with no content, or when there's nobody on the client side to receive and execute the plan. In those cases, [an agency that handles everything](/en/blog/como-fazer-seo-para-pequenas-empresas-um-guia-completo/) makes more sense, at least until the business matures.

In the Brazilian market, the most common profile hiring consulting is SMBs with meaningful digital revenue (e-commerce, SaaS, [professional services](/en/blog/o-poder-do-schema-org-para-empresas-de-servicos-um-guia-completo-para-o-seo-local/)) that have moved past the "I need a website" phase and reached the "I need this website to work for me" phase. It's also common among larger companies that already have an agency but feel the work lacks strategic direction.

## How do you know if you need consulting or just execution?

The distinction is simpler than it looks. Answer two questions:

Do you know exactly what needs to be done on your site, and you're just missing someone to do it? Then it's an execution problem. Hire a freelancer, an agency, or an in-house analyst.

Do you know something's wrong but not what, or know what to do but not where to start? Then it's a diagnosis-and-prioritization problem. Consulting.

In practice, many projects start with consulting and later shift to execution. The consultant diagnoses, builds the plan, follows the first months of implementation, and once the internal team gains confidence, the consulting engagement scales down or ends. That cycle is healthy. Consulting that never ends is usually an execution problem disguised as strategy.

## What should you expect from good SEO consulting?

Solid consulting delivers, at minimum, three things: a clear diagnosis of the current state (backed by data, not guesswork), a prioritized action plan (what to do first, second, third), and [implementation follow-through](/en/blog/como-monitorar-desempenho-seo-seu-site-ferramentas-gratuitas/) (periodic check-ins to adjust course as the data comes in).

What it doesn't deliver: a guarantee of page one. Any consultant who promises a specific Google position is selling something they don't control. [Google is a system with hundreds of variables](/en/blog/como-melhorar-o-ranking-do-seu-site-no-google-dicas-praticas/), and no serious professional promises an exact result. What gets promised is method, prioritization, and follow-through.

It also doesn't deliver results in 30 days. Organic SEO runs on cycles of 3 to 6 months for technical changes to take effect, and 6 to 12 months for content strategies to show consistent traction. If someone promises fast results, they're either talking about specific technical fixes (which can indeed have an immediate effect) or overselling.

The most reliable sign of good consulting isn't the logo wall on the website. It's how clearly the consultant explains what they're going to do, why in that order, and what happens if it doesn't work. Transparency of method is the best indicator of competence.
